# Why I Built AIMS

One thing that always struck me about Ultima Online was that, while players could develop all sorts of skills for their characters, there weren't many opportunities for the person behind the keyboard to demonstrate their own artistic talents.

Anyone could grind their way to Grandmaster in an in-game skill. But composing music is different. It's something the player brings to the game — their creativity, their musical ability, and their passion.

I wanted to give those players a way to express themselves within Ultima Online.

Early on, we discovered that OSI had exposed the individual notes of the game's musical instruments. To the best of my knowledge, those notes had never been made available to players as a means of performing actual music.

In 2006, Rhiannon built a simple player to see whether we could do something with them, with fixes from Pix and me. It had rudimentary timing, but it worked. For the first time, we could play recognizable melodies using Ultima Online's own instruments.

That was the beginning.

Over the years, I continued working on the system, trying to make it more expressive and give composers greater control over their music. In 2022 I rewrote it into a real instrument for composers, with control over tempo, timing and instruments within a song, and then gave their music somewhere to go: composition books, sheet music anyone could play, and music boxes where songs could be shared and sold. As players began experimenting with it, their ideas helped shape its development. One of those contributors was Galois, who earned the title of Grandmaster Composer through his published compositions and music sales, and whose feedback helped us improve the system.

What followed was something wonderful. Players began producing genuinely beautiful music, some of it remarkably complex. We even held a Battle of the Bards, where composers could share and celebrate their work. You can see some of what our composers accomplished in our [Battle of the Bards](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GCqLpk5YO1c).

That was when I felt we'd accomplished what I'd originally hoped for.

AIMS had become more than a way to play notes. It had become a way for real musicians to bring their talents into a virtual world.

And that's still what motivates its development today: giving the human behind the keyboard an opportunity to express something that no amount of in-game skill grinding could ever reproduce.

## Why I'm sharing it

I'm sharing AIMS in the hope that musicians on other shards will have the same opportunities ours have enjoyed. Ultima Online has been around for more than 25 years, and I'd like to see its music continue to grow, wherever players choose to make their home.

If you run it, I ask one thing: keep the names and the history that came with it. When people hear a song, they should know whose work they're enjoying — both the composer who created the music and the people who made the system possible.

— Luke Tomasello, aka Adam Ant
